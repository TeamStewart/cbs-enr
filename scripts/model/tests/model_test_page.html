How the TX precinct model behaves as votes come in (Bridget)

The full precinct model (scripts/model/tx_gov_trial.R) run on Ethan's simulated election nights, one scenario at a time. Each chart shows the model's estimate of the Democratic two-party share, its 95% interval, the raw share of votes counted so far, and the true final result.

    Simulated nights

    How each scenario went in the 32 counties

    Each row is one race in one run: one of Ethan's scenarios run through one model: model_y is the full precinct model (tx_gov_trial.R), model_simpleswing is the standard uniform swing model (feols with county fixed effects), and model_cory is Cory's model_legacy() from the enightmodels package. model_cory gives point estimates only, so it has no coverage, "confidently wrong" count or interval width. "What's different" lists the sim settings that differ from tx_base and the model settings that differ from the defaults. Coverage counts the checkpoints where the 95% interval held the true result; "confidently wrong" counts checkpoints where the whole interval was on the wrong side of 50%. Average error and interval width are over checkpoints with any votes counted, in percentage points of the two-party share; these are where runs with different model settings usually differ.

    One scenario in detail: the 32 counties

    Ethan's simulation produces the final count for all 6,134 precincts and a reporting schedule by vote mode: early votes in one countywide dump after polls close, Election Day precincts over the next few hours, and late mail days later. Some precincts never appear (unmatched IDs) and some counties may never report, as set in the scenario's manifest. The model is refit every 15 minutes from 8:00pm to 4:00am, then at four later times. Before any votes are in for a vote mode, model_y has nothing to fit for that mode, so the first one or two checkpoints show very wide intervals; model_simpleswing has no estimate at all until votes arrive.

    Times are Eastern. The time axis is not to scale after 4:00am: the last four points are Nov 4 noon, Nov 5, Nov 7 and Nov 10, when late mail lands. Counted share tops out below 100% because unmatched precincts and non-reporting counties never come in; the model predicts their votes from the baseline.

    Older test · simpler model, real 2022 results

    Older backtest: 2022 governor in our 32 counties

    The model is fit to the real 2022 result using only information available before November 2022: 2020 Senate results as the partisan baseline, 2018 turnout, and L2 demographics. Precincts are then revealed in order and the estimate is checked against the known result at each step. There are no 2022 precinct results by vote mode, so model_y can't run on it; this test uses the simpler model_core.R version and treats each precinct as one unit. It is kept for the reporting-order comparison.

    "Small counties first" reveals whole counties from smallest to largest, so Harris, Dallas, Bexar and Travis come last. That is harsher than a real night, where early votes from every county arrive within about an hour, and it isolates one problem: the first counties in are rural and Republican, so the model can read their lean as a statewide pattern. Compare the first two buttons to see what the county effect adds.

    What was run

    How these numbers were produced

    • Older backtest model (shown only if its results exist): the shared fitting functions in model_core.R (turnout, third-party share and two-party share equations; g-prior worth 3 precincts; county random effect with ICC 0.302), with the same covariates as tx_backtest.R: registrants, 2018 and 2020 turnout, sex, age 65+, Black, Hispanic and college shares.
    • Older backtest: test_backtest_tx22.R rebuilds Young-Kyung's panel (as in tx_backtest_panel.R) from the Dropbox files: precincts in the 32 counties joined to votehub's 2022 results. 500 draws per checkpoint.
    • Simulated nights: test_sim_night.R, once per scenario; the settings for each are under its chart. model_y is the full model in tx_gov_trial.R (precinct × vote-mode cells, bounded turnout, county random effect, cross-mode correlation, mode offsets): its own code builds the precinct frame, mode priors and cells, and it is fed the simulated counts at each time, with each cell flagged complete once that mode is fully counted; "core" is the simpler model_core.R version with no cross-mode correlation or mode offsets. Priors come from TX_precinct.parquet and the 2022 mode mix in TX_precinct_mode_history.parquet.
    • model_simpleswing, the standard uniform swing model: test_sim_night.R --model=swing. Each reported precinct × vote-method cell's swing in Democratic two-party share from its Harris 2024 share is regressed on county and vote-method fixed effects with fixest::feols, weighted by expected votes; unreported cells get their county's swing, and counties with nothing in yet get the average swing. Turnout is fit the same way on fully counted cells (log of counted over expected votes). The interval comes from 100 bootstrap resamples of the reporting counties. It has no estimate before any votes are in, so its runs start at the first checkpoint with votes.
    • model_cory: test_sim_night.R --model=cory calls enightmodels::model_legacy() exactly as the package provides it, with its default configuration (model_legacy_config(): by vote method, strata = geostratum, a precinct counts as complete at stage 6). Turnout: each stratum × method's ratio of counted to expected votes in reporting precincts, applied to the rest. Vote share: each candidate's ratio of counted votes to expected votes, by stratum and method. Its input is the standard election-data format built from Ethan's snapshot (one row per precinct × method × candidate, stage 0 / 4 / 6), with past set to the expected votes for that row (expected turnout × 2022 method split × the Harris 2024 two-party share, third parties from oth_base). It returns point estimates only.
    • Stress scenarios (made with make_stress_scenario.R from tx_base; Ethan's files are not changed): tx_hispanic_swing moves Hispanic voters 21.1 points of the two-party vote toward the Democrat on top of tx_base (from Trump winning Texas Latinos by about 10 in 2024, Democratic two-party about 45%, to 2026 polling of roughly 60–63% to 30–33%, about 66%). Each precinct moves by its Hispanic share of voters (YouGov's estimate) times 21.1 points, with its two-party total unchanged. tx_urbanfirst keeps tx_base's results but, within each county, has Election Day votes report from the most Democratic precincts first (by Harris 2024 share, standing in for urban cores, since the data has no urbanicity measure); each county keeps its own report times.
    • Limits: the simulation's swing, noise sizes, mail lean and timing are invented, and its results are built from the same dem2 the model uses as a prior, so its good performance after 9pm is partly by construction. So these runs test how the model copes with reporting order, timing, missing data and swings it doesn't expect; they can't show whether its intervals are the right width for real elections. That calibration check is Young-Kyung's TX 2024 backtest (tx24_backtest.R).
    Next

    What this suggests

    • Intervals need to widen when the counted precincts don't resemble the outstanding ones. One option is a geographic effect above the county (region or urban/rural) with a prior wide enough that the first counties can't pin it.
    • Turnout: on the simulated night model_y's predicted total for the 32 counties runs about 3–5% above the truth by the end of the night, with an interval far too narrow to cover it (see the turnout chart). The two-party share is unaffected, but turnout intervals should not be published until this is fixed.
    • To do: map this onto the statewide result properly. The statewide chart above is a first pass: it predicts the other 222 counties' swing from their demographics, using the relationship in the 32 counties, and never uses their returns. A real version should model the rest of the state from its county-level returns as they come in, carry the uncertainty of both parts through jointly, and be tested against a simulation that includes statewide truth.
