How the TX precinct model behaves as votes come in (Bridget)

Three election-night models run on Ethan's simulated night (tx_base) and two stress versions of it: model_medsl, the full precinct model (scripts/model/model_turnout.R); model_simpleswing, the standard uniform swing model; and model_cory, Cory's model_legacy() from enightmodels. Blue boxes: every 95% interval held the truth at 90%+ of checkpoints and was never confidently wrong; red: at least one race fell short; yellow: no interval to check. Each chart shows the model's estimate of the Democratic two-party share, its 95% interval, the raw share of votes counted so far, and the true final result.

Turnout is the 32-county total at 4am against the truth. All in the 32 counties except the last row.

    Simulated nights

    One scenario in detail: the 32 counties

    Ethan's simulation produces the final count for all 6,134 precincts and a reporting schedule by vote mode: early votes in one countywide dump after polls close, Election Day precincts over the next few hours, and late mail days later. Some precincts never appear (unmatched IDs) and some counties may never report, as set in the scenario's manifest. The model is refit every 15 minutes from 8:00pm to 4:00am, then at four later times. Before any votes are in for a vote mode, model_medsl has nothing to fit for that mode, so the first one or two checkpoints show very wide intervals; model_simpleswing has no estimate at all until votes arrive.

    Times are Eastern. The time axis is not to scale after 4:00am: the last four points are Nov 4 noon, Nov 5, Nov 7 and Nov 10, when late mail lands. Counted share tops out below 100% because unmatched precincts and non-reporting counties never come in; the model predicts their votes from the baseline.

    Calibration

    How often is each model wrong? Many synthetic nights

    Each synthetic night keeps tx_base's turnout, vote-method split and reporting schedule but draws a new result for every precinct: a race-wide shift (the 32-county result lands within about ±4 points of tx_base's), a shift for each county sized from real Texas swings, a within-county tilt by Hispanic, college and Black share (Hispanic from −15 to +20 points per 100% Hispanic, drawn per night), and a leftover precinct pattern taken from a real past Texas swing (2020→2022, 2022→2024 or 2020→2024) with its county and demographic parts removed. About three-quarters of the variation is within counties, as in real Texas swings. A well-calibrated model's 50%, 80% and 95% intervals hold the true 32-county result about 50%, 80% and 95% of the time. "Confidently wrong" is the share of race-nights where the whole 95% interval was on the wrong side of 50%. The 50% and 80% intervals are read off each model's 95% interval assuming a normal shape.     Scenario summary

    How each scenario went in the 32 counties

    Each row is one race in one run: one of Ethan's scenarios run through one model: model_medsl is the full precinct model (model_turnout.R), model_simpleswing is the standard uniform swing model (feols with county fixed effects), and model_cory is Cory's model_legacy() from the enightmodels package. model_cory gives point estimates only, so it has no coverage, "confidently wrong" count or interval width. Average error and interval width are over checkpoints with any votes counted, in percentage points of the two-party share; these are where runs with different model settings usually differ.

    What was run

    How these numbers were produced

    • Simulated nights: test_sim_night.R, once per scenario; the settings for each are under its chart. model_medsl is the full model in model_turnout.R (precinct × vote-mode cells, bounded turnout, county random effect, cross-mode correlation, mode offsets): its own code builds the precinct frame, mode priors and cells, and it is fed the simulated counts at each time, with each cell flagged complete once that mode is fully counted; "core" is the simpler model_core.R version with no cross-mode correlation or mode offsets. Priors come from TX_precinct.parquet and the 2022 mode mix in TX_precinct_mode_history.parquet.
    • model_simpleswing, the standard uniform swing model: test_sim_night.R --model=swing. Each reported precinct × vote-method cell's swing in Democratic two-party share from its Harris 2024 share is regressed on county and vote-method fixed effects with fixest::feols, weighted by expected votes; unreported cells get their county's swing, and counties with nothing in yet get the average swing. Turnout is fit the same way on fully counted cells (log of counted over expected votes). The interval comes from 100 bootstrap resamples of the reporting counties. It has no estimate before any votes are in, so its runs start at the first checkpoint with votes.
    • model_cory: test_sim_night.R --model=cory calls enightmodels::model_legacy() exactly as the package provides it, with its default configuration (model_legacy_config(): by vote method, strata = geostratum, a precinct counts as complete at stage 6). Turnout: each stratum × method's ratio of counted to expected votes in reporting precincts, applied to the rest. Vote share: each candidate's ratio of counted votes to expected votes, by stratum and method. Its input is the standard election-data format built from Ethan's snapshot (one row per precinct × method × candidate, stage 0 / 4 / 6), with past set to the expected votes for that row (expected turnout × 2022 method split × the Harris 2024 two-party share, third parties from oth_base). It returns point estimates only.
    • Stress scenarios (made with make_stress_scenario.R from tx_base; Ethan's files are not changed): tx_hispanic_swing moves Hispanic voters 21.1 points of the two-party vote toward the Democrat on top of tx_base (from Trump winning Texas Latinos by about 10 in 2024, Democratic two-party about 45%, to 2026 polling of roughly 60–63% to 30–33%, about 66%). Each precinct moves by its Hispanic share of voters (YouGov's estimate) times 21.1 points, with its two-party total unchanged. tx_urbanfirst keeps tx_base's results but, within each county, has Election Day votes report from the most Democratic precincts first (by Harris 2024 share, standing in for urban cores, since the data has no urbanicity measure); each county keeps its own report times.
    • Limits: the simulation's swing, noise sizes, mail lean and timing are invented, and its results are built from the same dem2 the model uses as a prior, so its good performance after 9pm is partly by construction. So these runs test how the model copes with reporting order, timing, missing data and swings it doesn't expect; they can't show whether its intervals are the right width for real elections. That calibration check is Young-Kyung's TX 2024 backtest (tx24_backtest.R).
    Next

    What this suggests

    • Intervals need to widen when the counted precincts don't resemble the outstanding ones. One option is a geographic effect above the county (region or urban/rural) with a prior wide enough that the first counties can't pin it.
    • Turnout: on the simulated night model_medsl's predicted total for the 32 counties runs about 3–5% above the truth by the end of the night, with an interval far too narrow to cover it (see the turnout chart). The two-party share is unaffected, but turnout intervals should not be published until this is fixed.
    • Statewide intervals. The statewide chart uses CBS's county returns for the other 222 counties as they come in (live/R/counties.R) and is scored against simulated statewide truth. After 11pm its intervals run somewhat narrow (see the phase table under the chart). Calibrating them properly needs many simulated nights with different rest-of-state swings, not three.
